BRIDGING THE GENERATION GAP

Family life is not always peaceful. Many teenagers argue with their parents because they have different opinions about studying, fashion and spending free time. Parents, (21) ________ grew up in a very different world, often worry that their children use smartphones too much. Teenagers, on the other hand, think that they should be allowed to make their own decisions. As a result, small arguments sometimes turn into serious conflicts at home.

These differences are called the generation gap, and they are quite common in most families. They usually happen because each generation grows up in different conditions. For example, grandparents often depend (22) ________ newspapers and television for information, while teenagers get news from the Internet. (23) ________ the two generations have different habits, they can still understand each other if they try. Neither side is completely right or wrong.

So how can families narrow the gap? First, both sides should listen to each other patiently and avoid shouting. Second, parents should show (24) ________ for their children's choices, and teenagers should value the experience of older people. Third, family members should spend more time doing activities together, such as cooking meals, going on trips or playing sports. These activities help everyone become closer and create (25) ________ bonds between family members.

In short, the generation gap is not a serious problem if everyone is willing to listen, understand and share their feelings with patience and love.

CITIES OF THE FUTURE: BUILDING SMARTER AND GREENER

By 2050, nearly seven out of ten people are expected to live in urban areas. This rapid growth puts enormous pressure on housing, transport and natural resources. Instead of simply building taller and bigger, urban planners around the world are now rethinking what a city should look like and how it should function.

One of the most important changes is in the way people travel. Many cities are expanding their metro networks and replacing diesel buses with electric ones, while some European cities have introduced car-free zones in their centers. These measures have greatly reduced noise and air pollution, making streets quieter and safer for pedestrians.

Buildings are changing as well. Green architecture, such as roofs covered with plants and walls fitted with solar panels, helps cut energy consumption and keeps interiors cool without air conditioning. Meanwhile, parks and vertical gardens are being added to crowded neighborhoods. They provide fresh air and places to relax, which improves residents’ well-being.

Technology plays an equally important role. Many cities now use sensors to monitor traffic and air quality. They allow authorities to adjust traffic lights in real time and warn residents when pollution reaches dangerous levels. In addition, mobile apps enable citizens to report problems, such as broken streetlights, directly to local officials.

However, a city of the future should not only be efficient but also fair. Experts stress that affordable housing and public services must be available to all residents; otherwise, modern facilities will benefit only wealthy people. Only when technology serves everyone can a city be truly livable.
